SCENE CONTENT
1. Terrain: a large ground grid (about 120 m x 120 m, e.g. 160x160 vertices, or finer near the camera) whose height is a smooth function h(x,y) of a few summed sines / smooth noise giving gentle rolling hills of ~1-3 m amplitude. Define h(x,y) as a Python function and USE THE SAME FUNCTION to place everything (grass, flowers, trees) on the surface. Material: green with slight variation (Principled BSDF with a Noise-texture-driven color ramp, roughness 0.9). Keep a flat-ish area near the camera path.
2. Distant hills: 2-3 layers of big smooth low-poly hills at 60-150 m away, getting bluer / paler with distance (atmospheric perspective look via colors), so the horizon looks layered.
3. Trees: 5-8 stylized trees (brown cylinder/cone trunk + a few merged icospheres or cones for foliage, low-poly, flat or smooth shading), different sizes, some at 15-50 m, a couple big ones near the sides of the view.
4. Grass: many blades. Build one blade (a thin tapered 3-4 triangle strip, bent a little) and place a LOT of them (target 25,000-40,000 blades at most ~12 triangles... keep total triangles under ~400k), denser near the camera path, with random rotation, scale, height 0.25-0.6 m and slight color variation per blade (use vertex colors or an Object-Info/Random-driven color ramp in the material, from dark green at the base to light yellow-green at the tip). Merge the blades into a few chunk meshes (e.g. a grid of patches such as 6x6 patches, each one object) so the animation can differ per patch.
5. Flowers: 80-200 small flowers (stem + a flat 5-petal or small icosphere head) scattered in clusters, colors white, yellow, pink, light purple (several materials), standing in the grass, positioned on h(x,y).
6. Sky: World with the procedural Nishita Sky texture (sky_type='NISHITA' in 4.2 ShaderNodeTexSky, sun elevation about 35-45 degrees, with air/dust default) OR a vertical gradient sky; plus several low-poly clouds: each cloud is a cluster of 4-8 white merged icospheres (smooth, flattened a bit, emission-free white diffuse material with a bit of subsurface-free look), floating at z about 60-120 m, spread across the sky in front of the camera.
7. Lights: one Sun light (Lights collection) with warm-white color, strength ~3-4, aimed from upper-left behind the camera so the grass is lit and casts shadows; the world provides the ambient light. If you use the Nishita sky, keep its sun disc consistent with the Sun lamp or disable the sky's own sun disc (sun_disc=False) so there are not two suns.
8. Camera (Camera collection): perspective, focal length about 28-32 mm, at eye height ~1.3-1.8 m above ground near the front edge of the meadow, looking slightly down towards the horizon so the frame shows: grass and flowers in the foreground, rolling meadow in the middle, trees and layered hills at the back, sky and clouds in the top 40% of the frame. The still should look like a nice postcard landscape.

ANIMATION (frames 1..144 at 24 fps = 6 seconds, seamless loop desired; set scene.frame_start=1, frame_end=144; set interpolation to LINEAR for loop-friendly motion; for looping make animated values at frame 145 equal to frame 1, i.e. use frame 1 and frame 145 keyframes with a 144-frame period or use cyclic F-curve modifiers):
  a) Wind: the grass must sway in the wind in a way that is visible in the final video: e.g. each grass patch gets a Wave-style deformation, or shape keys (Basis + 'sway' keys whose values are keyframed with sine-like motion and a different phase offset per patch), or armature/bone based, or an animated Simple Deform (BEND) modifier per patch with the angle keyframed; choose whichever you can make work reliably in a headless render. The deformation must bend the blades from the base (the base of each blade stays fixed on the ground, tips move most; the movement amplitude should be clearly visible, e.g. tips move 10-20 cm) and it should be a periodic 144-frame cycle with different phase per patch so waves seem to travel across the meadow. Flowers should sway a bit too (they can share the same technique or be given a small rotation animation). It is a plus if the sway can be exported to glTF as animation (shape-key/morph or object/bone animation); if it cannot, just note it in a comment.
  b) Clouds: slowly drift across the sky (linear motion of the cloud objects along X or a direction across the view, small total distance e.g. 8-15 m over the 144 frames); it does not need to loop perfectly.
  c) Camera: slow gentle forward dolly (and/or gentle pan), e.g. moves forward about 3-5 m with a slight sideways drift and tiny up/down bob over 144 frames (can end at a different place than it starts).  Use keyframes on the camera object or on a parent Empty with a Track To / fixed rotation. Keep it slow (a calm walk).
  d) Sun color / sky: the Sun lamp color and strength shift slightly over the clip (e.g. from neutral warm white (1.0,0.95,0.85) to slightly more golden (1.0,0.82,0.6) and back to the start at frame 145 so it loops), keyframed.
